## Limitations

In addition to the [networking limitations that apply to all of Compute](/compute/limitations-and-roadmap#networking), networks have the following limitations:

- Each workload attaches to exactly one network through one network interface, and it can't move to another network.
- Instance addresses aren't reachable from the internet. To serve traffic publicly, attach an [ALB](/alb/overview) to the workload.
- The `networkPolicy` field of a network interface has no effect. Don't rely on it to restrict traffic. Firewall rules are a planned capability.
- You can't choose a network's address ranges or create your own subnets.
- Networks carry Compute traffic only in the locations where Compute runs: `us-central-1`, `us-east-1`, and `us-west-1`.
- Every network can reach the internet, and you can't turn that off.
- Outbound internet traffic leaves from addresses that are shared with other Datum customers. Datum doesn't publish them, so a destination can't allowlist your Instances by address.
- Instances reach IPv4-only internet services by hostname only. A connection to an IPv4 address fails. See [Reach the internet](/galactic-vpc/networks/traffic#reach-the-internet).

## Planned capabilities

Datum is working on the following networking capabilities:

- **Firewall rules:** Security groups that control the traffic to and from your Instances.
- **Internet access setting:** A setting that turns internet access on or off for every Instance on a network.

Outbound internet access, including IPv4 destinations, [Private DNS](/domain-dns/private-dns) names for Instances, and viewing networks in the Datum Cloud portal are already available. For details, see [Reach the internet](/galactic-vpc/networks/traffic#reach-the-internet).

For what Datum is building next across the platform, see the [Platform roadmap](/platform/roadmap).
